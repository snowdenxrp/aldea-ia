## Finding: what is and is not already established
### Established as governance/design decisions
- Kevin is the sole initial constitutional authority under the intended Constitution.
- Initial commissioning requires explicit approval bound to the exact Constitution and commissioning context.
- A pre-existing, independently recognized channel under Kevin's control is accepted as a bounded design assumption to examine.
- The candidate Nexo instance and its provider/model/local state cannot create the authority that legitimizes them.
- Unknown enrollment legitimacy, presentation integrity, currentness/revocation, dependency closure, or enforcement blocks the dependent transition.

### Not established as deployment facts
- Which pre-existing channel credential is recognized before Nexo commissioning, or the evidence by which it was enrolled.
- How Core can authenticate the approval evidence without trusting a caller-supplied statement or an uncommissioned component.
- How Kevin sees the canonical Constitution and how that exact presentation is bound to the submitted content.
- Which source establishes credential currentness/revocation and governed recovery/migration.
- A protected Core authority-source implementation that can establish Constitution Authority Context from legitimate inputs.
- An independently evidenced final enforcement boundary.

## Architectural consequence
The first missing premise is narrower than “which phone is secure?” and earlier than the PolicyContext resolver:

**For the initial commissioning claim, what already-existing recognition/enrollment fact lets Core treat a response from the candidate channel as Kevin's bounded approval evidence, without letting the candidate Nexo/channel create its own legitimacy?**

The owner has accepted the assumption as a design premise, but that is not evidence of the channel's technical enrollment. The next design artifact should make this premise explicit as an input precondition and specify the evidence obligation and UNKNOWN/HOLD outcome when absent. It must not pretend to solve that precondition with a new API, generated key, hash, self-signature, app label, or a device audit.

This is a design-only clarification of the existing Genesis Trust Foundation / initial verifier contract. Before implementation, it must be attacked against self-enrollment, substitution, replay, currentness/revocation, dependency sharing, recovery, and faithful presentation using the already recorded attack cases. Do not duplicate the generic attacks.
