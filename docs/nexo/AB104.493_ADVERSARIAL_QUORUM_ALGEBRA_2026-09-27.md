# AB104.493 — adversarial tests of the quorum algebra

## Research
Dynamic Byzantine reconfiguration research shows that stale configurations can create conflicting information unless the transition protocol preserves the required quorum relation across configurations. citeturn0search0turn0search2 Byzantine quorum systems require more than ordinary set intersection: the relevant intersection must contain enough non-faulty participants for the protected property. citeturn0search3turn0search9 Recent heterogeneous-quorum work further shows that quorum intersection and availability alone may be insufficient for deterministic consensus under heterogeneous fault models. citeturn0search14

## Adversarial matrix

1. **Correlated failure:** many witnesses share one dependency. Raw weight can satisfy `T_E`, while effective independence collapses. Result: no promotion when dependency closure violates `I_E`.

2. **Weighted capture:** attacker controls high-weight members but not many identities. Numeric member count remains large, but authority weight and independence are compromised. Result: evaluate compromised weight and failure domains, not count.

3. **Stale epoch:** a valid old certificate arrives after a new fence. Cryptographic validity does not restore authority. Result: `epoch < current_epoch => historical_only`.

4. **Conflicting configurations:** two candidate epochs each satisfy their local quorum rule. Without a serialized transition/fence, both could appear valid. Result: conflicting activation evidence => `UNKNOWN_AUTHORITY`/quarantine.

5. **Hidden dependency masking:** witnesses appear geographically/operator-diverse but share a common key custodian, identity provider, deployment controller, or governance root. Result: independence is computed over dependency closure, not labels.

6. **Compromised intersection:** old/new quorums intersect only in compromised members. Ordinary intersection is insufficient. Result: require enough non-compromised overlap OR a trusted fence that removes old authority.

7. **Threshold downgrade under outage:** old quorum becomes unavailable and operator lowers `T_E`. Result: availability loss cannot silently modify safety parameters.

8. **Recovery-root capture:** all proposed recovery witnesses descend from the compromised trust basis. Result: `UNKNOWN_AUTHORITY`; no automatic recovery.

## Stronger candidate predicate

For a candidate quorum `S` in epoch `E`:

`SAFE_QUORUM(E,S) :=
  weight(S) >= T_E
  AND independence(S) >= I_E
  AND all_members_valid(S,E)
  AND trust_basis_valid(E)
  AND checkpoint_continuity_valid(E)
  AND fence_revision(S) >= current_fence`.

For two configurations, safety requires either a sufficient non-compromised relation between their quorums or an explicitly committed trusted fence that prevents the old configuration from authorizing after transition. This is stronger than raw intersection.

## New invariants
- `RAW_MEMBER_COUNT != EFFECTIVE_INDEPENDENCE`
- `RAW_WEIGHT != TRUSTED_WEIGHT`
- `CRYPTO_VALIDITY != CURRENT_AUTHORITY`
- `ORDINARY_INTERSECTION != HONEST_INTERSECTION`
- `COMPROMISED_INTERSECTION != SAFE_INTERSECTION`
- `STALE_EPOCH_CERTIFICATE => HISTORICAL_ONLY`
- `CONFLICTING_ACTIVE_EPOCHS => UNKNOWN_AUTHORITY`
- `COMMON_DEPENDENCY => POSSIBLE_COMMON_MODE_FAILURE`
- `AVAILABILITY_LOSS != SAFETY_PARAMETER_CHANGE`
- `RECOVERY_BASIS_SHARING_COMPROMISED_ROOT => NO_AUTOMATIC_RECOVERY`

## Boundary
No final numeric threshold is selected. Thresholds remain claim- and failure-model-specific. The algebra must be evaluated against explicit dependency/failure-domain graphs before any authority claim is made. No implementation or formal verification performed.
