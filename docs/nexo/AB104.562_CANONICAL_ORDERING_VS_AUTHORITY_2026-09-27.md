# Nexo AB104.562 — Canonical ordering versus authority

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: RESEARCH COMPLETE — DESIGN INPUT ONLY

## Evidence
RFC 9162 defines consistency proofs between two signed tree heads; the proof establishes that the later tree contains the earlier tree's inputs, not that either tree is authorized to control an external system. citeturn0search0 HotStuff safety instead depends on protocol-defined locking, ancestry and quorum intersection; a QC is meaningful because the protocol defines what a valid QC authorizes. citeturn0search1turn0search3

## Finding
A deterministic canonical ordering can safely resolve presentation/evaluation order, but it cannot by itself manufacture authority.

Safe uses:
- deterministic serialization/canonical encoding;
- stable ordering of evidence for comparison;
- selecting which conflict record to inspect first;
- deterministic audit/replay order;
- deduplication of identical evidence;
- tie-breaking only where the protocol has already proven that either choice is semantically equivalent and authority-safe.

Unsafe use:
- choose the "newer" certificate solely by timestamp;
- choose larger counter without an authenticated monotonic chain;
- choose lexicographically smallest/largest successor as authority;
- choose certificate with more signatures without checking epoch, independence and quorum intersection;
- turn a canonical sort key into an authority rule that was never part of the protected transition contract.

## Formal boundary
Define:
CANONICAL_ORDER(E) -> deterministic representation/order of evidence.
AUTHORITY_ADMISSION(C, S) -> protocol predicate deciding whether certificate C is sufficient for state S.

Authority must depend on AUTHORITY_ADMISSION, not CANONICAL_ORDER.

If two conflicting certificates are both valid evidence but neither uniquely satisfies AUTHORITY_ADMISSION, result is CONFLICTED/UNKNOWN/QUARANTINED.

If the protocol explicitly defines a total order as part of its safety proof and proves that competing candidates cannot both satisfy protected authority, then that order may participate in admission. The proof obligation is the important part; determinism alone is insufficient.

## Why this matters
RFC 9162 shows that consistency proofs can establish append-only relation between signed states. It does not imply that the latest signed state is the correct Nexo authority. Likewise, a HotStuff QC has authority only within its protocol's quorum/locking semantics.

## Critical distinctions
CANONICAL_ORDER != AUTHORITY
DETERMINISTIC != SAFE
NEWER != AUTHORIZED
HIGHER_COUNTER != COMMITTED
MORE_SIGNATURES != MORE_INDEPENDENCE
CONSISTENCY_PROOF != AUTHORITY_PROOF
PRESENTATION_TIE_BREAK != SECURITY_TIE_BREAK

## Nexo consequence
Nexo should keep canonicalization in the evidence/verification layer and keep authority admission in Z1. A canonical order may make conflict handling reproducible, but an unresolved authority conflict must remain UNKNOWN/QUARANTINED unless a pre-proven protocol rule establishes a unique safe outcome.
