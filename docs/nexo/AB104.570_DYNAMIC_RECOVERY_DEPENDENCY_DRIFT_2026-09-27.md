# Nexo AB104.570 — Dynamic dependency drift during recovery

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: RESEARCH COMPLETE — DESIGN INPUT ONLY

## Evidence
Byzantine dynamic-reconfiguration research explicitly treats a configuration as superseded only after a higher configuration is installed with sufficient quorum evidence, and requires state-transfer correctness before the new configuration becomes active. It also notes that tentative configurations may contain Byzantine replicas that have not yet updated their keys, so transitional safety must not assume instantaneous trust transfer. citeturn0search12 Raft joint consensus likewise keeps old/new configurations constrained during transition and requires both configurations for agreement. citeturn0search0turn0search24

## Finding
Recovery authority is not a static object. Its dependency closure can drift while recovery is executing.

Examples:
- a recovery root is revoked;
- an HSM/TEE becomes unavailable or changes firmware/attestation state;
- a shared identity provider becomes compromised;
- two previously independent sites become dependent on the same control plane;
- a witness loses synchronization with the trusted continuity boundary;
- a provider capability or evidence source expires;
- a dependency changes epoch while a recovery certificate is being assembled.

Therefore a quorum that was admissible at preparation time may be inadmissible at activation time.

## Required rule
Recovery must use a coherent dependency snapshot captured at admission and revalidated at the final activation boundary.

Candidate sequence:
DEPENDENCY_SNAPSHOT_0
→ RECOVERY_PREPARED
→ DEPENDENCY_REVALIDATION
→ RECOVERY_CERTIFICATE
→ FINAL_AUTHORITY_GATE
→ RECOVERY_ACTIVE

If the dependency closure changes incompatibly before FINAL_AUTHORITY_GATE, invalidate the prepared recovery decision and re-evaluate. Do not silently reuse the old certificate.

## Drift classes
SAFE_EXTENSION: dependency evidence becomes stronger without changing the authority assumptions.
REVOCATION: a required root/capability/evidence source is no longer valid.
CORRELATION_DRIFT: two roots acquire a common failure dependency.
EPOCH_DRIFT: authority or witness epoch changes.
STATE_DRIFT: recovered state no longer matches the protected continuity boundary.
EVIDENCE_EXPIRY: freshness/coverage assumptions lapse.
UNKNOWN_DRIFT: the system cannot establish whether the dependency changed safely.

Only SAFE_EXTENSION can potentially preserve the prepared decision automatically. Other classes require revalidation; UNKNOWN_DRIFT blocks promotion.

## Byzantine implication
A compromised participant can deliberately create stale or misleading dependency evidence during recovery. Therefore dependency evidence itself needs epoch, freshness, provenance and continuity binding. A certificate signed before a dependency revocation cannot automatically override the revocation.

## Critical distinctions
PREPARED_RECOVERY != ACTIVE_RECOVERY
FRESH_COMPONENTS != FRESH_COMPOSITION
OLD_CERTIFICATE != CURRENT_AUTHORITY
DEPENDENCY_CHANGE != HARMLESS_CHANGE
REVOCATION != HISTORICAL_ERASURE
UNKNOWN_DRIFT != SAFE_DRIFT

## Nexo consequence
The dependency snapshot becomes part of the recovery certificate and final authority gate. This directly reuses AB104.504 dynamic dependency closure and AB104.533 temporal capability composition. Recovery authority must be revalidated immediately before activation; otherwise a valid preparation can cross an invalidated trust boundary.
