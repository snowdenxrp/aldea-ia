# Nexo AB104.553 — SAFE_GC revocation and UNKNOWN after the boundary

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: RESEARCH COMPLETE — DESIGN INPUT ONLY

## Finding
SAFE_GC is not a permanent truth about the world. It is a claim whose validity depends on explicit assumptions and evidence. Therefore Nexo must distinguish:
- GC_COMMITTED — a durable transition occurred;
- GC_SAFE_AT_COMMIT — required assumptions were valid at the decision boundary;
- GC_PROPERTY_REMAINS_SUPPORTED — the protected claim remains supportable under later observations.

Kafka and etcd demonstrate that compaction irreversibly removes some historical information after a retention boundary. Kafka explicitly gives delete.retention.ms as a bound for consumers to complete a scan before tombstones may be collected; etcd states that revisions before compaction become inaccessible.

## Key result
If an assumption becomes UNKNOWN after GC, do not retroactively declare the earlier GC unsafe without identifying a violated premise. But also do not treat the earlier GC as evidence that the protected property is still true.

Post-GC state:
1. SUPPORTED — current evidence still satisfies the claim.
2. INVALIDATED — a concrete fact contradicts a required premise/property.
3. UNKNOWN — evidence needed to distinguish supported from invalidated is unavailable.

UNKNOWN must not silently promote to SUPPORTED.

## Monotonicity
A SAFE_GC predicate should be monotonic only with respect to the evidence domain it was designed over. If a new observation reveals that the domain was incomplete (undiscovered relay, recovery path, provider retention window, or clock assumption), the prior proof was incomplete rather than repaired by the old timestamp.

The GC record should bind: exact predicate version; assumption set; dependency/coverage snapshot; provider capability version; target incarnation; authority epoch/fence; retention/delay bounds; recovery snapshot boundary; archival coverage; decision evidence digests.

## Revocation handling
If a dependency is later revoked: freeze further GC for affected classes; prevent reuse that depends on the revoked barrier; create an INVALIDATION/REVIEW record; re-evaluate which historical claims remain provable; quarantine if protected-effect safety cannot be reconstructed.

A revocation does not itself prove that a previously GC'd message executed. It changes the epistemic status of claims that depended on the revoked assumption.

## Adversarial example
T0: Nexo computes SAFE_GC(H) using max-delay D=1h and provider fence enforcement E=true.
T1: H is garbage-collected.
T2: provider evidence E is revoked because its durable high-water mark was discovered not to survive restore.
T3: delayed H appears.

Nexo cannot conclude H definitely executed, and cannot conclude it definitely did not execute. Correct state: EFFECT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN / RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED unless independent evidence closes the uncertainty.

## Required distinctions
SAFE_GC_AT_T0 != FOREVER_SAFE
GC_COMMITTED != HISTORICAL_NONOCCURRENCE
REVOKED_ASSUMPTION != PROOF_OF_PRIOR_EXECUTION
UNKNOWN_AFTER_GC != SAFE
UNKNOWN_AFTER_GC != PROVEN_FAILURE

## Nexo design consequence
Every GC decision becomes an auditable protected transition with claim-specific dependencies. Preserve enough metadata after deletion to determine which claims depended on the deleted artifact. Evidence GC and execution-state GC must be modeled separately.
