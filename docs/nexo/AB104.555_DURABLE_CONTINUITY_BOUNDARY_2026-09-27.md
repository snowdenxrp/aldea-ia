# Nexo AB104.555 — Durable continuity boundary

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: RESEARCH COMPLETE — DESIGN INPUT ONLY

## Evidence
etcd documents that snapshot restore can move revisions backward; its recovery procedure can bump revisions and mark them compacted so old watches/caches are invalidated. Restore also overwrites member/cluster identity and starts a new logical cluster. This is concrete evidence that recovery needs both a monotonic continuity mechanism and an identity boundary. citeturn0search0turn0search6

## Finding
The minimum durable continuity boundary for Nexo cannot be just a snapshot revision or timestamp. It must bind at least:
- protected authority epoch;
- monotonic continuity counter/version;
- state snapshot identity/digest;
- target/resource incarnation information where relevant;
- active fence high-water marks or an authoritative statement of their reconstruction;
- recovery/restore generation;
- integrity/authenticity evidence;
- dependency/coverage snapshot for claims that survived GC.

Candidate object: ContinuityAnchor.

## Required property
A restored state may be used for reconstruction, but it cannot acquire normal protected authority unless it proves it is compatible with the latest trusted ContinuityAnchor. If the anchor is older, missing, equivocated, or cannot be independently authenticated, recovery enters QUARANTINED/UNKNOWN.

## Why a timestamp is insufficient
A wall-clock timestamp does not establish ordering under clock rollback, skew, or restore. A local counter also fails if it can be rolled back with the snapshot. Therefore the continuity boundary must itself survive the recovery mechanism or be independently witnessed.

## Recovery rule
SAFE path:
VALIDATE_ANCHOR -> VALIDATE_SNAPSHOT -> ESTABLISH_NEW_RECOVERY_EPOCH -> INVALIDATE_PRE_ANCHOR_WRITERS -> RECONCILE_OVERLAPPING_EFFECTS -> RELEASE

Unsafe path:
RESTORE_OLD_SNAPSHOT -> ASSUME_CURRENT -> RESUME_AUTHORITY

## GC implication
If GC removes an artifact used to reject stale messages, the ContinuityAnchor must survive that GC. It acts as the durable boundary saying that all pre-anchor material is obsolete for admission, while separate archival evidence may still be required for historical claims.

## Trust-domain compromise
A continuity anchor stored only inside the compromised trust domain cannot independently prove that the domain did not roll back or rewrite it. Therefore the strongest recovery claim may require an independent witness/root or equivalent anti-rollback mechanism. If independence is absent, the status remains UNKNOWN rather than promoted.

## Critical distinctions
CONTINUITY_ANCHOR != SNAPSHOT
CONTINUITY_ANCHOR != WALL_CLOCK
MONOTONIC_COUNTER != ROLLBACK_PROOF
NEW_RECOVERY_EPOCH != HISTORICAL_ERASURE
ANCHOR_VALIDITY != EXTERNAL_EFFECT_RECONCILIATION
ANCHOR_WITNESS != EXECUTION_AUTHORITY
INDEPENDENT_WITNESS != AUTOMATIC_TRUTH

## Nexo consequence
ContinuityAnchor should be treated as a claim-specific protected object, not ordinary memory. Its lifecycle needs enrollment, update, recovery, witness validation, rollback detection, and decommission semantics. It must not silently grant authority to a restored system.
