# Nexo AB104.416 — Byzantine Witnesses, Equivocation and Collusive Quorum Attack

## Research question

How should Nexo classify and contain witnesses that intentionally sign conflicting generations/roots, replay old evidence, collude across nominal failure domains, or selectively withhold observations?

## Evidence

RFC 9334 defines the Verifier as the role that appraises Evidence and produces Attestation Results, while the Relying Party applies its own policy when deciding whether to trust/use those results. It also warns that freshness has a race with changes in the attested state or appraisal policy. citeturn0search0turn0search36

RFC 9162 provides a concrete transparency-system example: monitors audit append-only behavior and consistency, while inconsistent views can require cross-observer comparison. It explicitly notes that a malicious log can present different views to different clients, showing why multiple signed observations are not automatically a safe resolution. citeturn0search1turn0search2

## Core finding

A Byzantine witness is not merely STALE.

It may actively attempt to create contradictory evidence:

W1 signs ROOT_A at generation G
W1 signs ROOT_B at generation G

or:

W1 tells Z1 ROOT_A
W1 tells W2 ROOT_B

Therefore:

MULTIPLE_VALID_SIGNATURES != ONE_VALID_STATE

and:

SIGNED_CONFLICT => EQUIVOCATION_CANDIDATE

## Byzantine behavior classes

### B1 — equivocation

Same protected generation, different roots or state claims.

Candidate:

SAME_GENERATION + CONFLICTING_ROOT
=> EQUIVOCATION / DISPUTED

Do not select the majority root merely because it has more signatures.

### B2 — selective omission

Witness withholds a relevant revocation, generation, or conflicting observation.

Missing report does not mean no event occurred.

NO_OBSERVATION != OBSERVATION_OF_NO_EVENT

### B3 — replay

Witness repeatedly supplies a previously valid observation after its generation has expired.

Candidate:
- verify generation;
- verify freshness;
- verify invalidation generation;
- reject stale current-use evidence.

### B4 — fork amplification

A witness signs ROOT_A for one quorum and ROOT_B for another.

If those signatures are accepted independently, Nexo can manufacture two apparently valid histories.

Candidate:
- bind witness contribution to an authority domain and continuity generation;
- retain contradictory signed evidence;
- mark affected witness/history disputed.

### B5 — collusion

Multiple witnesses coordinate to create the same false view.

Therefore:

QUORUM_COUNT != INDEPENDENCE

and:

N witnesses sharing a common compromised dependency may have effective Byzantine strength of one failure domain.

### B6 — strategic silence

A malicious witness responds only when its response helps satisfy quorum.

Availability policy must not interpret silence as affirmative evidence.

### B7 — equivocation after recovery

A witness signs one recovery generation before partition healing and another after.

Recovery must compare predecessor/continuity relationships rather than trusting the newest timestamp.

## Witness contribution binding

Each contribution should be bound to:

- WITNESS_ID
- WITNESS_INCARCATION
- WITNESS_GENERATION
- AUTHORITY_DOMAIN
- CONTINUITY_GENERATION
- OBSERVED_ROOT
- OBSERVED_PREDECESSOR
- OBSERVATION_SEQUENCE
- CLAIM/SCOPE
- TRUST_GENERATION
- DEPENDENCY_ROOT
- POLICY_VERSION
- FRESHNESS/EPOCH
- EVIDENCE_ID

This prevents a valid signature from being detached from the state it was supposed to witness.

## Equivocation evidence

Candidate minimum proof:

EQUIVOCATION_PROOF =
two authenticated witness statements
+
same witness identity/incarnation
+
same protected generation/scope
+
incompatible state claims

A single contradictory statement without a comparable context may be insufficient to prove equivocation; it may instead be STALE, MISBOUND, or CORRUPT evidence.

## Equivocation response

Candidate state machine:

CURRENT
→ SUSPECTED_EQUIVOCATION
→ EVIDENCE_CAPTURED
→ EQUIVOCATION_CONFIRMED / DISPUTED
→ CONTRIBUTION_BLOCKED
→ DEPENDENCY_IMPACT_ANALYSIS
→ AFFECTED_QUORUMS_REEVALUATED
→ EXTERNAL_EFFECT_RECONCILIATION
→ REINSTATEMENT or REVOKED/QUARANTINED

Historical evidence is preserved.

## Why arbitrary majority is unsafe

Suppose five witnesses report:

A, A, A, B, B

A majority exists.

But if A-witnesses share one compromised provider and B-witnesses are independent, the count alone says nothing about truth.

Therefore:

MAJORITY_COUNT != INDEPENDENT_EVIDENCE_WEIGHT

No universal numeric threshold is selected here.

## Failure-domain weighted quorum

A candidate policy may constrain the maximum number of contributing witnesses sharing each protected failure domain.

Example conceptually:

- require multiple independent trust domains;
- require independent provider/deployment domains;
- prohibit all quorum members from sharing one critical policy/verifier root.

This is a policy candidate, not a selected numeric rule.

## Common-mode Byzantine attack

A malicious common dependency can cause honest witnesses to emit the same false result.

Example:

W1 and W2 use independent storage but the same compromised policy source.

Both honestly execute the compromised policy.

Their signatures remain authentic.

Their observations remain correlated.

Therefore:

AUTHENTIC_WITNESS != INDEPENDENT_WITNESS

and:

HONEST_EXECUTION != CORRECT_SEMANTICS

This carries forward AB104.397–AB104.404.

## Cross-witness contradiction

If W1 says:

GENERATION=G, ROOT=A

and W2 says:

GENERATION=G, ROOT=B

then Nexo must distinguish:

1. one stale witness;
2. delayed replication;
3. legitimate separate observation scope;
4. actual equivocation;
5. compromised witness;
6. common-mode corruption;
7. unknown.

Do not immediately label one side malicious without sufficient evidence.

Security-critical unresolved contradiction:

DISPUTED / QUARANTINED

## Byzantine witness and freshness

A witness can sign a fresh message containing stale state.

Therefore:

FRESH_MESSAGE != FRESH_OBSERVATION

The signed generation/root and continuity state must be checked, not only the message timestamp.

RFC 9334 explicitly notes that freshness only narrows recentness and that state/policy may change immediately after evidence generation. citeturn0search0

## Byzantine witness and recovery

Recovery must not allow a witness to certify its own restored history.

Candidate rule:

RECOVERY_EVIDENCE_FROM_DISPUTED_WITNESS
cannot independently establish
CURRENT_CONTINUITY.

A recovery anchor must use an independent continuity mechanism whose own trust/dependency closure is evaluated.

## Replay across generations

A valid observation from G must not satisfy a requirement for G+1.

Candidate:

OBSERVED_GENERATION != REQUIRED_GENERATION
=> STALE / REJECTED

A timestamp cannot replace this generation binding.

## Byzantine witness replacement

If W1 is compromised and replaced by W1' using the same logical ID, the replacement must have a new incarnation/generation and undergo revalidation.

Candidate:

SAME_LOGICAL_ID + NEW_INCARCATION
!= SAME_TRUST_STATE

## Evidence preservation

When equivocation is detected:

- preserve both signed statements;
- preserve receipt/observation metadata;
- preserve trust/dependency generations;
- preserve the decision context;
- preserve affected quorum evaluations;
- do not rewrite the historical record to make the conflict disappear.

## Candidate Byzantine admission rule

A quorum contribution is eligible only if:

1. witness enrollment is current;
2. witness identity/incarnation is current;
3. trust/key generation is current;
4. dependency closure is current;
5. claim scope matches;
6. continuity generation is current;
7. observation is bound to the required root/predecessor;
8. freshness is satisfied;
9. no confirmed equivocation invalidates the contribution;
10. required independence/failure-domain policy is satisfied;
11. final protected generations remain unchanged.

Otherwise:
- REJECT;
- REVALIDATE;
- HOLD;
- DISPUTE;
- QUARANTINE;
as dictated by the unresolved condition.
