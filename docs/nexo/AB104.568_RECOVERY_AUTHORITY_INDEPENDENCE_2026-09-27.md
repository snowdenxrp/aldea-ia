# Nexo AB104.568 — Independence of recovery authority

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: RESEARCH COMPLETE — DESIGN INPUT ONLY

## Evidence
NIST SP 800-57 treats compromise recovery as requiring replacement of affected keying material and recognizes backup/recovery as a distinct lifecycle function. It also distinguishes backup storage from operational authority and discusses split knowledge and trust-anchor protection. citeturn0search24turn0search25turn0search4

## Finding
Recovery independence cannot be established by one property alone. Geographic separation, different administrators, different hardware, or a different key are useful only if they do not share a common failure/compromise dependency that defeats the claimed trust basis.

Candidate dimensions:
- CRYPTOGRAPHIC: separate keys and trust roots;
- ORGANIZATIONAL: separate control/administrative authority;
- PHYSICAL: separate sites and physical access paths;
- HARDWARE: separate root-of-trust or cryptographic boundary;
- SOFTWARE: independent implementation/control plane where required;
- NETWORK: separate connectivity/provider dependencies;
- HUMAN: distinct recovery custodians/roles and separation of duties;
- TEMPORAL: recovery material not simultaneously exposed to the operational compromise;
- EVIDENCE: independent source for the continuity boundary being recovered.

## Correlated-failure rule
Two recovery roots count as independent only to the extent that the protected claim does not depend on a common compromise domain that can forge, suppress, or rewrite both.

Examples:
COPY_COUNT != INDEPENDENCE
DIFFERENT_KEYS != DIFFERENT_TRUST_ROOTS
DIFFERENT_SITES != DIFFERENT_FAILURE_DOMAINS
DIFFERENT_ADMINS != INDEPENDENT_AUTHORITY
BACKUP != CURRENT_AUTHORITY

A backup in another region that shares the same cloud control plane, identity provider, signing root, or administrator may still be one trust domain for the relevant claim.

## Recovery admission
A candidate recovery authority should therefore carry a dependency closure:
RecoveryRoot -> key material -> hardware/crypto boundary -> administrators -> infrastructure/provider -> network -> continuity evidence.

If two roots share a dependency that is capable of compromising the recovery claim, their apparent threshold must be reduced accordingly. If no sufficient independent set remains, recovery cannot promote authority.

## Human bootstrap
A human recovery role can be an independent authority only if its credentials, authentication path, governance, and evidence are outside the compromised domain. Merely naming a human as an emergency approver does not create independence.

## Hardware roots
A separate HSM/TEE/secure element can strengthen separation, but hardware independence is claim-specific. If the same vendor firmware, provisioning root, operator, or remote-management authority can compromise both, the hardware count does not equal trust-root independence.

## Nexo consequence
Recovery authority should be represented as a dependency graph/hypergraph, not just a numeric threshold. Admission evaluates independence against the specific protected claim. A failure-domain/common-mode analysis must precede any recovery promotion.
