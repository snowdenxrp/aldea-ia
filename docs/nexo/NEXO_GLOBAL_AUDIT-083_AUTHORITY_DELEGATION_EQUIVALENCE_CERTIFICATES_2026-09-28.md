# NEXO GLOBAL AUDIT-083 — Authority, Delegation and Composition of Equivalence Certificates

Date: 2026-09-28
Repository: snowdenxrp/aldea-ia
Branch: main

## Scope

Audit-083 attacks equivalence-proof authority and composition: delegation, certificate chains, shared proof engines, revocation, expiry, epoch transitions, conflicting certificates, replay after authority loss, quorum composition, and FutureObs_PAA.

No implementation. No V21. No semantic freeze.

## Findings

### 1. Proof authority is a separate dependency

A mathematically valid equivalence proof does not establish that its issuer was authorized to certify the relevant claim.

TUF gives a concrete analogue: a root role delegates trust to keys for roles, delegated roles can further delegate, and delegation can later be revoked by updated metadata. The authority path is therefore part of the verification state, not an attribute that can be ignored after signature validation.

AUTHENTIC PROOF != AUTHORIZED PROOF
PROOF VALIDITY != PROOF AUTHORITY

### 2. Delegated proof authority

A delegated certificate inherits only the authority explicitly granted by its parent under the relevant scope and interval.

A child certificate cannot silently expand:
- claim scope;
- observer contract;
- historical interval;
- source population;
- semantic relation;
- authority epoch.

TUF explicitly scopes delegated roles to target paths and permits further delegation, illustrating that delegation is a bounded trust relation rather than universal authority.

DELEGATION != ROOT AUTHORITY
CHILD PROOF != BROADER PARENT PROOF

### 3. Certificate chains

A chain of individually valid certificates does not automatically form a valid semantic chain.

Each edge introduces dependencies:
issuer authority, scope, epoch, target identity/incarnation, predicate/claim semantics, and revocation state.

A missing or ambiguous edge leaves the composed certificate UNKNOWN rather than being filled by the next certificate.

VALID EDGES != COMPLETE CHAIN
CHAIN LENGTH != AUTHORITY COMPLETENESS

### 4. Shared proof engines

Two independently signed equivalence certificates generated by the same proof engine, same reducer, same source, or same checkpoint can remain common-mode evidence.

A different signer only changes authentication lineage unless the underlying proof dependencies also differ.

DIFFERENT SIGNERS != INDEPENDENT PROOFS
MULTIPLE PROOF OUTPUTS != MULTIPLE PROOF ROOTS

### 5. Revocation of proof authority

Revoking an issuer/delegation changes current admissibility of certificates whose authority depends on that issuer, but it does not erase the historical fact that the certificate was issued and accepted under the earlier authority state.

This mirrors TUF's explicit root/delegation rotation and revocation model.

AUTHORITY REVOCATION != HISTORICAL ERASURE
CURRENT AUTHORITY != HISTORICAL AUTHORITY

If historical-invalidity semantics are separately defined, they must be proven and scoped rather than inferred from current revocation.

### 6. Expiry

Expiry can terminate current trust without proving that the certified historical relation was false when issued.

An expired certificate may remain relevant as historical evidence or as an input to reconstruction/rollback analysis.

EXPIRY != RETROACTIVE FALSEHOOD
EXPIRED-FOR-ADMISSION != ERASURE

### 7. Epoch transitions

A new authority epoch must not automatically inherit all equivalence certificates from the previous epoch.

Continuity requires explicit transition semantics binding:
- old authority;
- new authority;
- affected scope;
- source incarnation;
- certificate validity interval;
- inheritance/non-inheritance rule.

EPOCH CHANGE != AUTOMATIC SEMANTIC CONTINUITY

### 8. Conflicting equivalence certificates

Two authentic certificates can disagree because they may refer to different:
- observer contracts;
- claim scopes;
- source incarnations;
- reducer versions;
- historical intervals;
- authority epochs.

A deterministic tie-breaker cannot manufacture semantic precedence when authority/scope semantics are unresolved.

AUTHENTIC CONFLICT != RESOLVED CONFLICT
DETERMINISTIC TIE-BREAK != JUSTIFIED PRECEDENCE

TUF provides an important bounded example: delegated roles can overlap target responsibility and prioritized delegation can define explicit ordering. That ordering is part of TUF's specified semantics; it cannot be generalized into a universal precedence rule for Nexo.

### 9. Historical replay after authority loss

Replaying an old certificate after its issuer has been revoked or replaced must preserve the distinction between:
- what was accepted historically;
- what is currently admissible;
- whether the historical decision itself is subject to retroactive invalidation.

A replay engine that substitutes today's authority for the historical authority can silently rewrite history.

CURRENT ROOT != HISTORICAL ROOT
REPLAY UNDER CURRENT AUTHORITY != RECONSTRUCTION OF HISTORICAL ADMISSION

### 10. Quorum composition

A quorum of certificates can be authentic and still share:
- one issuer;
- one delegation root;
- one proof engine;
- one source;
- one checkpoint;
- one reducer;
- one population authority.

Therefore quorum composition must recurse through certificate dependency graphs.

QUORUM OF CERTIFICATES != INDEPENDENT PROOF QUORUM
THRESHOLD AUTHENTICATION != INDEPENDENT SEMANTIC SUPPORT

### 11. FutureObs_PAA

Authority closure, certificate composition, revocation, expiry and quorum can bound the validity of a historical certificate under explicit contracts.

They do not establish that no future observation can invalidate or distinguish the claim.

AUTHORITY CLOSURE != FUTURE FINALITY
CERTIFICATE FINALITY != FUTUREOBS_PAA CLOSURE

## Research-only composition boundary

A composed equivalence certificate would need to bind at minimum:

- certificate identity;
- issuer and delegation chain;
- authority epoch;
- scope and observer contract;
- source identity/incarnation;
- transformation/reducer lineage;
- historical interval;
- equivalence/refinement relation;
- proof engine/version;
- dependency closure;
- revocation/expiry state;
- reconstruction/loss status;
- conflict status;
- common-mode dependencies.

This remains research-only and is not a frozen Nexo protocol.
