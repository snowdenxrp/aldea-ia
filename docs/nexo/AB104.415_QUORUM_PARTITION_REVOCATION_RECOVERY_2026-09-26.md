# Nexo AB104.415 — Quorum Under Partition, Delayed Revocation, Witness Disagreement and Recovery

Date: 2026-09-26
Status: RESEARCH / DESIGN ONLY
Continuity: AB104.414 → AB104.415
Implementation: NOT PERFORMED

## Research question

What is the safe behavior when quorum witnesses see different generations, roots, revocation states, or trust information during a partition or recovery event?

## Evidence

RFC 9334 separates Verifier appraisal from Relying Party authorization and treats freshness as a property that must be checked against policy and current state. A relying party cannot safely treat an old attestation result as indefinitely current. citeturn0search2

RFC 9162 demonstrates that append-only consistency can be checked with consistency proofs, but also documents the possibility of inconsistent views and the need for independent monitoring/comparison. Importantly, auditing can be asynchronous, so detection may lag the underlying event. citeturn0search0

## Core finding

Partition creates an epistemic split:

W1 sees generation G
W2 sees generation G+1
W3 is disconnected
Z1 sees incomplete revocation state

A quorum count alone cannot determine which view is authoritative.

Therefore:

QUORUM_COUNT != CURRENT_AUTHORITY

and:

MAJORITY_OF_STALE_VIEWS != CURRENT_STATE

## Partition classes

### P1 — witnesses stale, Z1 current

Z1 knows the current protected generation.

Candidate:
- stale witness evidence is excluded;
- no downgrade to old generation merely to obtain quorum.

### P2 — Z1 stale, witnesses disagree

Candidate:
- no high-consequence promotion;
- obtain a current protected anchor/recovery decision;
- unresolved disagreement => UNKNOWN/HOLD.

### P3 — Z1 and witnesses partitioned from current authority

Candidate:
- protected authority cannot be assumed from local majority;
- enter degraded/quarantined mode;
- only operations whose safety does not depend on the missing authority may continue.

### P4 — witnesses disagree on same generation

Same generation + different state roots:

ANCHOR_EQUIVOCATION

Candidate:
- DISPUTED/QUARANTINED;
- no arbitrary majority selection.

### P5 — witnesses differ only by generation

A higher generation is not automatically authoritative unless its predecessor/continuity relationship is validated.

Candidate:
- verify generation ordering + predecessor binding + continuity evidence;
- otherwise UNKNOWN.

## Revocation during partition

Suppose W1 is revoked but W2 cannot observe the revocation.

W2 may continue producing apparently valid signatures.

Candidate distinction:

SIGNATURE_VALID
!=
CURRENT_WITNESS_ELIGIBILITY

If the revocation generation is not known to the partitioned verifier, it cannot prove current eligibility.

For security-critical authorization:

UNKNOWN_REVOCATION_STATE => HOLD / REVALIDATE

unless policy explicitly defines a bounded safe degraded mode.

## No silent fail-open

The following are unsafe defaults for protected quorum:

- reduce threshold;
- accept older generation;
- accept cached witness status indefinitely;
- treat unreachable witness as valid;
- treat missing revocation as no revocation;
- choose the majority root;
- assume the highest generation is correct;
- reuse an old quorum evaluation.

Candidate invariant:

AVAILABILITY_FALLBACK_CANNOT_WEAKEN_PROTECTED_SAFETY_PROPERTY

## Bounded degraded operation

Availability can be modeled explicitly rather than implicitly.

Candidate states:

NORMAL
→ DEGRADED
→ QUORUM_UNSATISFIED
→ HOLD
→ REVALIDATION_REQUIRED
→ RECOVERING
→ CURRENT

A degraded mode may only execute operations whose effect class/property remains safe under the missing information.

For example, an E0 observation may remain possible while an E4 irreversible effect is blocked.

This preserves the earlier E0–E5 effect-class distinction.

## Quorum membership is protected state

Membership changes must not be treated as an ordinary availability operation.

Candidate fields:

QUORUM_POLICY_VERSION
QUORUM_THRESHOLD
WITNESS_SET_ROOT
WITNESS_GENERATION_SET
FAILURE_DOMAIN_RULE
TRUST_GENERATION
INVALIDATION_GENERATION

A change requires a new protected admission boundary.

## Split-brain attack

Two partitions each see enough local witnesses to satisfy a naive threshold.

Partition A:
W1, W2, W3

Partition B:
W4, W5, W6

If each partition can authorize independently, Nexo may produce two mutually incompatible authority histories.

Candidate protection:

A QUORUM IS VALID ONLY RELATIVE TO A UNIQUE PROTECTED AUTHORITY DOMAIN AND CURRENT CONTINUITY GENERATION.

Witness signatures alone cannot establish uniqueness.

## Recovery after partition

Recovery must not simply merge both histories.

Candidate:

RECOVERY_REQUEST
→ FREEZE_PROTECTED_EFFECTS
→ COLLECT_PARTITION_VIEWS
→ VERIFY_CONTINUITY
→ DETECT_EQUIVOCATION
→ RECONSTRUCT_DIVERGENT_EVENTS
→ IDENTIFY_EXTERNAL_EFFECTS
→ RECONCILE
→ ESTABLISH_NEW_RECOVERY_GENERATION
→ REVALIDATE_WITNESSES
→ EXPLICIT_RELEASE

If two conflicting histories cannot be reconciled:

QUARANTINED / DISPUTED

## Delayed revocation

If revocation becomes known after an authorization was committed:

- do not rewrite history;
- mark the historical decision with its effective generation;
- determine whether the resulting external effect remains recoverable;
- reconcile according to effect class;
- prevent further use of the revoked witness.

Thus:

REVOCATION_IS_NOT_RETROACTIVE

but:

REVOCATION_REQUIRES_FUTURE_ADMISSION_BLOCKING

## Ambiguous ordering

If the system cannot determine whether:

AUTHORIZATION
or
REVOCATION

occurred first at the protected linearization boundary:

ORDER_UNKNOWN

Candidate:
- do not invent an order;
- classify the decision/effect UNKNOWN;
- reconcile externally where necessary.

This carries forward the canonical rule:

MISSING_EVIDENCE != EVIDENCE_OF_ABSENCE

## Witness disagreement taxonomy

Disagreement must be classified before resolution:

- STALE_VIEW
- REPLICATION_LAG
- PARTITION
- DIFFERENT_VALID_GENERATION
- CONFLICTING_ROOT
- EQUIVOCATION
- WITNESS_COMPROMISE
- COMMON_MODE_FAILURE
- UNKNOWN

Only disagreements with a verified safe resolution path may leave HOLD/QUARANTINE.

## Recovery cannot self-authorize

A recovery process must not decide:

“the partitioned state says I am authoritative, therefore I am authoritative.”

Recovery needs an authority transition anchored outside the disputed state or an independently protected continuity mechanism, consistent with AB104.410–AB104.412.

## External effects during partition

Even if Z1 authorizes an operation locally, external providers may continue receiving stale requests.

Therefore partition handling must preserve:

- authority epoch;
- resource fence;
- EffectID;
- attempt identity;
- durable intent;
- provider-side fencing where required;
- reconciliation.

Local quorum safety does not prove external non-execution.

## Candidate decision rule

For a protected quorum decision:

ALLOW only if:

1. authority domain is current;
2. continuity generation is current;
3. quorum policy is current;
4. required witness set is satisfied;
5. every contributing witness is current for the claim;
6. dependency/failure-domain constraints are satisfied;
7. revocation/invalidation state is current;
8. no unresolved conflicting root exists;
9. final protected generations remain unchanged at the decision boundary.

Otherwise:

- REVALIDATE if state may become known safely;
- HOLD if currentness cannot be established;
- QUARANTINE if conflicting security-critical histories/evidence exist;
- RECONCILE if an external effect may already have occurred.

## Residuals

- Formal quorum protocol and exact consensus assumptions.
- Exact failure model (crash, omission, Byzantine, network partition).
- Numeric thresholds remain claim/policy dependent and are not selected.
- Recovery authority and independent continuity-anchor choice remain open.
- External provider behavior during partition remains resource-specific.
- Formal proof, implementation, fault injection, and long-duration testing remain unperformed.
