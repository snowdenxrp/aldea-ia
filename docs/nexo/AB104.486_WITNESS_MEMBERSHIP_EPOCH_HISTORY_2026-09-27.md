# AB104.486 — append-only witness membership epochs and rollback protection

## Evidence
RFC 9162 defines signed tree heads, append-only Merkle history, and consistency proofs between earlier and later tree heads. Its auditing model checks that current published state is reachable from previously known-good state; it also states that the response to detected log misbehavior is not itself specified. citeturn0search0turn0search3
RFC 9334 separates evidence appraisal from the relying party's authorization decision and treats trust anchors as authoritative for constrained types of information. citeturn0search1

## Nexo candidate model
Witness membership must therefore be an append-only governance history, separate from the transparency log's ordinary data history.

Candidate record:
`MembershipEpoch = {EpochID, ParentEpochDigest, EffectiveFromCheckpoint, Members, Threshold, TrustBasis, Revocations, PolicyVersion, CreatedAt, ActivationEvidence}`

Transition:
`E0 -> PROPOSED -> VALIDATED -> ACTIVATED -> SUPERSEDED`
with `REVOKED/QUARANTINED` as protected exceptional states.

The active epoch must point to an earlier accepted epoch and be bound to a checkpoint continuity relation. A new epoch cannot silently rewrite an old epoch, lower the safety threshold during recovery, or reactivate a revoked member.

## Rollback defense
Automatic rollback is permitted only to an explicitly authorized prior epoch and only when its checkpoint/history relation and authority basis remain valid. A stale membership snapshot is evidence of historical state, not current authority.

Required distinctions:
- `MEMBERSHIP_EPOCH != CHECKPOINT`
- `KEY_ROTATION != MEMBERSHIP_ROTATION`
- `MEMBERSHIP_ROTATION != TRUST_BASIS_ROTATION`
- `THRESHOLD_CHANGE != AUTOMATIC_AUTHORIZATION`
- `HISTORICAL_EPOCH != CURRENT_AUTHORITY`
- `SIGNED_EPOCH != VALID_CURRENT_EPOCH`
- `CONSISTENCY_PROOF != GOVERNANCE_AUTHORIZATION`

## Compromise boundary
If the old witness set is compromised, the transition must fence the old epoch before activating the new one. The new authority cannot be derived solely from signatures produced by the compromised epoch. A compromise discovered after activation must preserve the historical record while invalidating the affected epoch for future authorization; it must not erase earlier evidence.

## Candidate protected transition
`OLD_EPOCH_ACTIVE -> OLD_EPOCH_FENCED -> NEW_EPOCH_VALIDATED -> CHECKPOINT_CONTINUITY_VERIFIED -> NEW_EPOCH_ACTIVATED -> OLD_EPOCH_REVOKED`

The ordering is intentionally explicit: validation and continuity precede activation; revocation prevents old members from regaining authority. The exact atomicity required between fencing and activation remains an open design question for the clean architecture.
