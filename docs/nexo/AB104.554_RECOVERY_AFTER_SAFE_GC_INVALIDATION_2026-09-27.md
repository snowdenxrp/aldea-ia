# Nexo AB104.554 — Recovery after SAFE_GC invalidation

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: RESEARCH COMPLETE — DESIGN INPUT ONLY

## Evidence
etcd recovery documentation warns that restoring an older snapshot can make clients perceive revision going backwards; revision bump prevents decreasing revisions, and mark-compacted terminates watches so stale informer caches are invalidated. Restore also creates a new logical cluster identity. Historical revisions removed by compaction become inaccessible. citeturn0search0turn0search4

## Finding
If a SAFE_GC assumption is later invalidated, recovery must never simply restore an older state and resume normal authority. Recovery needs a new logical epoch/incarnation and must fence or invalidate pre-recovery writers and cached messages.

## Candidate recovery sequence
1. DETECT_INVALIDATION
2. STOP / BLOCK_PROTECTED_EFFECTS
3. establish RECOVERY_EPOCH
4. restore only from integrity-validated artifacts
5. compare snapshot revision/epoch/incarnation against the last durable continuity boundary
6. invalidate stale caches/watchers/relays
7. issue a new authority/fence generation
8. reconcile every protected external effect whose outcome may overlap the lost interval
9. retain QUARANTINED status for unresolved effects
10. explicitly release only after current authority, recovery fence, and reconciliation conditions are satisfied.

## Critical recovery rule
A restored snapshot is not automatically a continuation of the former authority timeline. If older than the last known protected boundary, it is candidate historical state, not current authority.

## Why this matters for GC
If an artifact was GC'd under a predicate later found invalid, the missing artifact cannot be reconstructed merely by replaying a stale snapshot. Replay may reproduce an old state while omitting later fences, incarnations, revocations, or external attempts. Therefore recovery must distinguish state reconstruction, authority reconstruction, external-effect reconstruction, and evidence reconstruction. They are separate claims.

## Adversarial example
T0 SAFE_GC(H) committed under epoch E10.
T1 H deleted.
T2 provider fence durability is discovered invalid after restore.
T3 system restores snapshot at E9.
T4 old relay H reappears.
Unsafe recovery could accept H because E9 does not know E10. Safe recovery first establishes a fresh recovery epoch E11 and rejects/quarantines all E<=E10 material until reconciliation closes the interval.

## Nexo rule
RESTORED_STATE != CURRENT_AUTHORITY
SNAPSHOT_REVISION != EFFECT_HISTORY_COMPLETENESS
RECOVERY_SUCCESS != EXTERNAL_EFFECT_RECONCILIATION
NEW_RECOVERY_EPOCH != PROOF_OF_OLD_EFFECT_NONOCCURRENCE
INVALIDATED_GC != PROOF_OF_EXECUTION

If the old barrier cannot be reconstructed and no independent evidence can close the historical interval, protected effects remain UNKNOWN/QUARANTINED rather than guessed.

## Design implication
Recovery must preserve a durable continuity boundary outside any artifact that may itself be GC'd. A recovery snapshot must carry enough lineage to prove whether it is ahead/behind the last authoritative boundary. If not, recovery starts quarantined and requires a new authority epoch plus reconciliation.
