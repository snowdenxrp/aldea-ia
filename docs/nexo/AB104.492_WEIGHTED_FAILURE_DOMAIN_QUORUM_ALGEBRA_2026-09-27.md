# AB104.492 — weighted/failure-domain quorum algebra

## Evidence
Dynamic Byzantine quorum research shows that changing quorum configurations is unsafe if stale configurations can continue to obtain conflicting information; safe adjustment rules must preserve the required correctness relation across configuration changes. citeturn0search12turn0search14 Classical Byzantine quorum systems require quorum intersections containing enough correct participants; one common b-masking formulation requires intersections of at least 2b+1 servers. citeturn0search13 Tendermint provides concrete weighted voting evidence: commits require more than two-thirds of total validator voting power, and validator-set updates are versioned into future heights rather than silently changing the current set. citeturn0search10turn0search0

## Nexo algebra
Represent each witness `i` as:
`w_i = (power_i, failure_domains_i, trust_basis_i, status_i, epoch_i)`.

For a configuration `E`, define quorum validity as a predicate over sets, not only a scalar threshold:
`Q(E) = {S | weight(S) >= T_E AND independence(S,E) >= I_E AND status(S,E)=VALID}`.

Where:
- `T_E` = cryptographic/governance voting threshold;
- `I_E` = minimum independent trust/failure-domain condition;
- `status` excludes fenced/revoked/unknown members;
- dependency closure is evaluated before counting independence.

## Three thresholds
`CRYPTO_THRESHOLD` answers whether a signature/aggregate is mathematically valid.
`GOVERNANCE_THRESHOLD` answers whether the configured decision rule was satisfied.
`INDEPENDENCE_THRESHOLD` answers whether enough genuinely independent trust/failure domains support the decision.

They must not be collapsed:
`CRYPTO_THRESHOLD != GOVERNANCE_THRESHOLD != INDEPENDENCE_THRESHOLD`.

A fourth dimension remains operational:
`AVAILABILITY_THRESHOLD` — whether enough admissible members are reachable now.
It must never silently lower the safety thresholds.

## Common-mode rule
Two witnesses with different keys/operators may still share a controlling dependency. Therefore:
`INDEPENDENCE(S) = function(failure-domain closure, trust-basis closure, governance closure)`.

Counting raw members is insufficient. A single common dependency that can forge, suppress, or coordinate the counted witnesses reduces effective independence.

## Transition condition
For old/new configurations, automatic transition is safe only if the selected transition protocol establishes either:
1. sufficient non-compromised overlap between admissible old/new quorums; or
2. a durable trusted fence that makes the old configuration unable to authorize after the fence, followed by an independently authorized new configuration.

Thus:
`OLD_NEW_INTERSECTION_SAFETY OR TRUSTED_FENCE_RECOVERY`
not simply `|Q_old ∩ Q_new| > 0`.

## Candidate Nexo predicate
`AUTHORIZE(E,S) :=
 epoch(S)=E
 AND weight(S)>=T_E
 AND independence(S)>=I_E
 AND all_members_valid(S,E)
 AND trust_basis_valid(E)
 AND checkpoint_continuity_valid(E)
 AND fence_revision(S)>=current_fence`.

If any protected predicate is UNKNOWN, authorization does not promote automatically.

## Invariants
- `MEMBER_COUNT != CRYPTO_THRESHOLD`
- `CRYPTO_THRESHOLD != GOVERNANCE_THRESHOLD`
- `GOVERNANCE_THRESHOLD != INDEPENDENCE_THRESHOLD`
- `AVAILABILITY_THRESHOLD != SAFETY_THRESHOLD`
- `WEIGHTED_QUORUM != INDEPENDENT_QUORUM`
- `COMMON_DEPENDENCY => EFFECTIVE_INDEPENDENCE_REDUCTION`
- `FENCED_MEMBER => ZERO_CURRENT_AUTHORITY`
- `UNKNOWN_INDEPENDENCE => UNKNOWN_AUTHORITY`
- `NO_SAFE_OLD_NEW_RELATION => REQUIRE_TRUSTED_FENCE`
- `QUORUM_LOSS != SAFETY_THRESHOLD_REDUCTION`

## Status
This establishes a candidate algebra and boundary conditions, not final numeric parameters. The exact failure-domain and dependency model remains open and must be claim-specific. No implementation or formal verification performed.
