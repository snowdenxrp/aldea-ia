# Nexo AB104.566 — Byzantine witnesses during JOINT_ACTIVE

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: RESEARCH COMPLETE — DESIGN INPUT ONLY

## Evidence
HotStuff safety depends on quorum intersection and protocol-defined certificate/locking rules; a valid certificate is meaningful only under those semantics. CCF's Byzantine configuration uses 2f+1 of 3f+1 and its reconfiguration model requires evidence tied to the configuration being changed. Research on asynchronous Byzantine reconfiguration shows that tentative configurations can contain Byzantine replicas that have not yet adopted the new key material, so transitional states must explicitly account for that fact rather than assuming immediate trust transfer. Generalized-quorum work also shows that simple numeric thresholds may be insufficient when independence/trust structures are more complex. citeturn0academia25turn0search26turn0search12turn0academia27

## Finding
During JOINT_ACTIVE, compromised old witnesses can simultaneously threaten safety and liveness:
- equivocate between old and new successor histories;
- withhold signatures and block progress;
- sign a certificate that is locally valid but lacks the required cross-configuration evidence;
- replay old-epoch certificates;
- exploit an incomplete state transfer to make the new set appear authoritative prematurely.

Therefore Nexo must not equate joint membership with simple union quorum.

## Minimum safe exit evidence
To promote NEW_ACTIVE, require a protocol-defined transition certificate proving:
1. predecessor/continuity anchor binding;
2. correct old and new witness-set epochs;
3. required quorum/intersection evidence from the joint transition;
4. independence/failure-domain conditions;
5. state/dependency snapshot compatibility;
6. no unresolved conflicting certificate with equal or higher authority;
7. activation phase is durably committed;
8. new witnesses are synchronized to the protected continuity boundary.

A complaint, timeout, or local suspicion is evidence for investigation, not by itself authority to remove an old witness. Recent reconfiguration research similarly uses certificates and sufficient multi-party support before exclusion rather than unilateral local suspicion. citeturn0search9

## Byzantine equivocation
If an old witness signs incompatible successors for the same transition context, preserve both signed statements and record EQUIVOCATION. The witness may be excluded from the affected transition's usable evidence according to the predefined protocol, but equivocation alone does not identify which successor is correct. The remaining quorum/intersection proof must still justify the transition.

## Liveness versus safety
A compromised old witness may make JOINT_ACTIVE unable to reach the configured threshold. The safe response is not to lower the threshold dynamically. Instead, use the predefined reconfiguration/recovery path or remain blocked/quarantined. Numeric threshold reduction would change the trust model and could invalidate previous safety assumptions.

## State-transfer boundary
A new witness must not become voting-authoritative merely because it has a valid identity or join certificate. It needs the required protected state/continuity evidence. This matches current reconfiguration research where a joining node remains non-voting until synchronization checks succeed. citeturn0search5

## Critical distinctions
JOINT_MEMBERSHIP != UNION_QUORUM
EQUIVOCATION != CORRECT_SUCCESSOR
TIMEOUT != REVOCATION_AUTHORITY
JOIN_CERTIFICATE != VOTING_AUTHORITY
THRESHOLD_REDUCTION != RECOVERY
IDENTITY_VALIDITY != STATE_SYNCHRONIZATION
LIVENESS_FAILURE != SAFETY_PERMISSION

## Nexo consequence
JOINT_ACTIVE must be an explicit protected state with its own quorum, independence, synchronization, and conflict predicates. NEW_ACTIVE requires a unique, durably committed transition certificate. If compromised witnesses prevent proving that boundary, Nexo remains UNKNOWN/QUARANTINED rather than weakening the trust model.
