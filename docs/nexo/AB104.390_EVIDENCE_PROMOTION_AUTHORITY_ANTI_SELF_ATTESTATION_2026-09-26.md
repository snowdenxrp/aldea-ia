## Research question
Who may promote provider capability evidence, what trust roots are required, and how should Nexo prevent a provider or adapter from self-attesting its own safety-critical semantics?

## External evidence
RFC 9334's RATS architecture separates the entity producing Evidence (Attester), the Verifier that appraises Evidence under an appraisal policy, and the Relying Party that uses Attestation Results for an application decision. It also describes trust anchors and freshness as explicit parts of the trust model. citeturn0search0turn0search24

RFC 9711 likewise describes signed claims, freshness, verification, and the production of attestation results consumed by a relying party. citeturn0search2

These architectures support Nexo's separation of:
EVIDENCE_PRODUCER != EVIDENCE_VERIFIER != AUTHORITY_DECIDER.

They do not prove Nexo's provider capabilities by themselves.

## Proposed roles

### 1. Evidence Producer / Attester
The provider, adapter, gateway, runtime observer, or test harness produces observations and artifacts.

It MUST NOT be sole authority for promoting its own safety-critical capability.

### 2. Capability Verifier
An independent or separately controlled component evaluates evidence against a claim-specific appraisal policy.

It checks:
- identity and scope;
- version/incarnation;
- declared semantics;
- positive and negative tests;
- failover/recovery evidence;
- evidence dependencies;
- contradictions;
- freshness;
- trust anchors;
- adapter path.

### 3. Nexo Relying Party / Authority
The protected authority plane consumes the verifier result and decides whether the capability may support a specific protected claim/effect class.

The verifier does not automatically grant operational authority.

### 4. Policy Owner
Defines what evidence is required for each capability/effect class and what trust roots are accepted.

Policy changes are themselves protected state and require versioning/authorization.

## Anti-self-attestation rule
A provider MAY report:
- "I support fencing";
- "I preserve ETags";
- "I deduplicate EffectID";
- "I preserve state across failover".

Those statements are evidence inputs, not final validation.

For a safety-critical capability, VALIDATED requires evidence whose trust does not reduce to the provider's own unverified assertion.

## Trust-root model

Candidate trust anchors:
- provider signing/identity keys;
- adapter build provenance;
- configuration integrity source;
- independent test environment identity;
- hardware/TEE attestation where applicable;
- reference semantics/documentation;
- independent observer identities.

A trust anchor authenticates the source; it does not automatically establish that the source's semantic claim is true.

Therefore:
AUTHENTIC_SOURCE != TRUE_SEMANTICS

## Promotion rule

A capability claim C may be promoted to VALIDATED only if:

1. Evidence source is authenticated to an accepted trust anchor.
2. Evidence is bound to exact provider/resource/incarnation/adapter/version scope.
3. The appraisal policy for C is current and authorized.
4. Required adversarial tests have passed.
5. Required failover/recovery tests have passed.
6. Evidence dependency graph has no unresolved common-mode contradiction for the claimed property.
7. Any provider self-assertion is corroborated by an independent evidence path when required by claim criticality.
8. Freshness/revocation requirements are satisfied.
9. The verifier produces a signed/durable AttestationResult-like record.
10. The authority plane separately decides whether that result is sufficient for the requested effect class.

## Independence classes

I0 — Same source/path:
provider assertion, provider telemetry, provider logs.

I1 — Different observation path, same provider:
independent adapter/observer but same provider failure domain.

I2 — Independent execution/observer:
separate harness/control path and independent evidence collection.

I3 — Independent trust domain:
evidence verified by an independently controlled verifier/trust root.

For low-risk properties, I0/I1 may be enough depending on policy.
For safety-critical stale-authority/failover claims, stronger independence should be required.

Exact thresholds remain open and claim-specific.

## Compromise handling

### Provider compromised
Provider evidence becomes untrusted for claims dependent on that provider. Existing VALIDATED capability is not automatically erased historically, but current status may become UNKNOWN/DEGRADED/QUARANTINED depending on the affected claim.

### Adapter compromised
Only claims whose evidence path depends on the adapter are invalidated. Other independently validated provider properties may remain scoped separately.

### Verifier compromised
Verifier output cannot be blindly trusted. The trust anchor/policy must support verifier replacement/revocation and re-appraisal.

### Policy owner compromised
Capability promotion policy itself becomes suspect. Protected authority should stop accepting newly promoted capabilities until policy integrity is restored.

### Observer compromised
Evidence from that observer is invalidated; independent paths may remain valid.

## Freshness and revocation
A capability result needs:
- issuance identity;
- evidence generation time/epoch or equivalent freshness mechanism;
- validity window/epoch;
- provider/API/adapter version;
- resource incarnation;
- capability profile version;
- verifier policy version;
- revocation/invalidation reference.

Freshness reduces stale-evidence risk but does not eliminate state-change races; a capability may change immediately after appraisal. RFC 9334 explicitly discusses freshness and the possibility of state changes after Evidence or Attestation Result generation. citeturn0search0

## Promotion state
UNVALIDATED
→ DOCUMENTED
→ OBSERVED
→ ADVERSARIALLY_VALIDATED
→ VERIFIED
→ AUTHORITY_ACCEPTED

Important separation:
VERIFIED != AUTHORITY_ACCEPTED

A verified capability result can still be rejected for a particular effect class or environment.

Contradiction/invalidation:
→ UNKNOWN / DEGRADED / QUARANTINED

Historical evidence remains immutable.
